Data Visualization — Final Project

Does free speech
build a negativity engine?

We mine engagement patterns from X/Twitter's most influential accounts to reveal whether open platforms systematically reward negative content — and compare that signal against coordinated Russian troll activity.

Research from Cambridge & Nature (2024) confirms the direction of our hypothesis: users are nearly twice as likely to share negative news articles than positive ones on social media.

Watson, J., van der Linden, S., Watson, M. et al. Negative online news articles are shared more to social media. Sci Rep 14, 21592 (2024)

“Although people produce much more positive content on social media in general, negative content is much more likely to spread.”

Amit Goldberg, assistant professor in the Negotiation Organization & Markets unit at Harvard Business School, after analyzing over 140 thousand tweets across various news channels.
Genre & Visualization Mockup
An Interactive Slideshow
that argues a point
Segel & Heer — Section 4.3
Our chosen genre is the Interactive Slideshow. This sits at the intersection of author-driven narrative and reader-driven exploration. We have a clear thesis: negative content earns disproportionate engagement. An author-driven structure lets us build the argument step by step, but readers need agency. Interactive filters let users interrogate the multi-dimensional data themselves.
Visual hierarchy strongly highlights the "Negative" sentiment bars, immediately anchoring the core thesis of the presentation.
Contextualizing aggregate data with real tweet examples makes the abstract numbers tangible and emotionally resonant.
Filters grant the user agency to explore specific cohorts (e.g., Trolls vs Politicians) and discover nuances in the dataset organically.
