Data Visualization — Final Project

Does free speech
build a negativity
engine?

We mine engagement patterns from X/Twitter's most influential accounts to reveal whether open platforms systematically reward negative content — and compare that signal against coordinated Russian troll activity.

What
Sentiment analysis + engagement-ratio modelling on tweets from world leaders, celebrities, and public figures. We measure like/retweet/comment ratios across positive and negative posts to find structural patterns.
Which Data
Dataset 1: Influential Figures tweets (Kaggle) — politicians, celebrities, public figures globally.

Dataset 2: Russian IRA troll tweet archive — for cross-dataset engagement comparison.
Why It Matters
If negative content consistently earns more engagement, the platform's algorithm rewards — and therefore incentivises — negativity. Comparing authentic influencers with manufactured troll content tests whether this effect is organic or engineered.

Research from Cambridge & Nature (2024) confirms the direction of our hypothesis: users are nearly twice as likely to share negative news articles than positive ones on social media. A UC Berkeley audit of Twitter's algorithm (2023, N=806) found it amplifies emotionally charged, out-group hostile content — content users themselves say makes them feel worse.

Segel & Heer — Section 4.3

Our chosen genre is the Interactive Slideshow. This sits at the intersection of author-driven narrative and reader-driven exploration — exactly what our story needs.


Why this genre? We have a clear thesis: negative content earns disproportionate engagement. An author-driven structure lets us build the argument step by step — first establishing the baseline, then revealing the sentiment split, then introducing the troll dataset as a twist.


But readers need agency. Engagement data is inherently multi-dimensional (likes vs. retweets vs. replies behave differently). Interactive filters let users interrogate the data themselves — making our claim more convincing than if we just showed one chart.


Pure annotation or partitioned poster genres would bury the comparative troll angle. A video wouldn't allow the filtering. The interactive slideshow threads the needle: guided narrative + free exploration.
